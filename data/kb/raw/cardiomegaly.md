# Cardiomegaly

Cardiomegaly means the heart appears enlarged. It is not a single disease by itself; it is a finding that can happen when the heart has changed because of another condition. Common associated problems can include high blood pressure, heart valve disease, coronary artery disease, cardiomyopathy, or heart failure.

The finding matters because an enlarged cardiac silhouette can be a clue that the heart is under strain or not pumping normally. Heart failure can cause symptoms such as tiredness, shortness of breath, and fluid buildup in the body, although the exact cause cannot be decided from a chest X-ray alone.

On a chest X-ray, cardiomegaly is usually considered when the cardiac silhouette is larger than expected compared with the chest. A common measurement is the cardiothoracic ratio on a PA chest radiograph; a ratio above about 50 percent can suggest enlargement. Portable AP views can exaggerate heart size, so the report should describe the finding carefully and consider image technique.

Cardiomegaly should be reported as an imaging observation, not as a full diagnosis. Follow-up with clinical history, prior imaging, echocardiography, or other tests may be needed to understand the cause.
