# Pleural Effusion

Pleural effusion means extra fluid has collected in the pleural space, which is the thin space between the lung surface and the inside of the chest wall. A small amount of fluid is normally present there, but too much fluid can make it harder for the lung to expand normally.

The fluid buildup can happen for different reasons. Inflammatory causes, infection, tumors, or lung injury can lead to an exudative effusion. Pressure-related causes, such as heart, kidney, or liver failure, can lead to a transudative effusion. The cause matters because the same X-ray finding can come from different underlying problems.

On chest X-ray, pleural effusion is usually considered when there is abnormal fluid at the lung base or in the pleural space. It may appear with blunting of the costophrenic angle, dependent layering fluid, or a horizontal air-fluid level if both air and fluid are present. Lateral decubitus views can show smaller amounts of fluid than an upright chest X-ray.

Pleural effusion matters because it can reduce lung expansion and may be associated with shortness of breath, chest pain, or cough. The report should describe the imaging finding carefully without pretending to know the exact cause from the X-ray alone.
