# Consolidation / Pneumonia

Pneumonia is an infection of the lungs. It can be caused by bacteria, viruses, fungi, or less common organisms. Symptoms may include cough, fever or chills, chest pain with breathing or coughing, tiredness, and shortness of breath.

Consolidation is a radiographic pattern where normally air-filled lung tissue becomes filled with material such as inflammatory fluid, pus, blood, edema, or other liquid. Pneumonia is one common cause of consolidation, but consolidation itself is an imaging sign and does not prove one exact cause by itself.

On chest X-ray, consolidation can appear as a focal or lobar air-space opacity. It may look denser or whiter than normal aerated lung. In pneumonia, the opacity may match the infected region, and air bronchograms can sometimes be seen when air-filled bronchi remain visible within surrounding opacified lung.

Consolidation should be described as an imaging pattern and interpreted with symptoms, history, and other clinical information. The report should describe the opacity pattern and avoid claiming the organism or exact diagnosis from the X-ray alone.
