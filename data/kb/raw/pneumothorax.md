# Pneumothorax

Pneumothorax means air or gas has collected in the pleural space, the space between the lung and chest wall. This air can put pressure on the lung and may cause part or all of it to collapse.

Pneumothorax can happen spontaneously, after trauma, or in people with underlying lung disease. A tension pneumothorax is especially important because pressure can build up enough to affect the heart, lungs, blood flow, and blood pressure.

On chest X-ray, pneumothorax is usually considered when a visible pleural line separates the collapsed lung edge from the chest wall, with absent lung markings beyond that line. Larger cases may show a more obvious collapsed lung. Tension physiology may be suggested by mediastinal shift or other signs of pressure effect, but clinical urgency should not wait for perfect imaging wording.

Pneumothorax is an important finding because it can explain sudden chest pain or shortness of breath and may require urgent action. A report should clearly describe the side and approximate size when visible.
