# Edema

Pulmonary edema means excess fluid in the lungs. It can be cardiogenic, often related to heart problems and increased pressure, or noncardiogenic, related to other lung or systemic injury. The important idea is that fluid interferes with normal air exchange.

Pulmonary edema can cause shortness of breath, cough, low oxygen, and respiratory distress. The cause cannot be decided from the chest X-ray alone, but the imaging pattern can help suggest whether edema is present and whether the distribution looks more typical of cardiogenic or noncardiogenic edema.

On chest X-ray, pulmonary edema may show increased vascular markings, Kerley B lines, perihilar or batwing air-space opacity, upper-lobe vascular redistribution, and sometimes pleural effusions. Patchier alveolar opacities may be seen in noncardiogenic edema or other lung injury patterns.

A report should describe the imaging pattern carefully, such as vascular congestion or pulmonary edema pattern, and should not overstate the underlying cause without clinical support.
